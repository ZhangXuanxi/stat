Review: how often will a test detect an effect?
For a fixed test and significance level α, its power function is
pow(θ) = Pθ(reject H0).
Under an alternative, this is the probability of a true positive; 1 − pow(θ) is the probability of a
type II error. Under a null parameter, the same rejection probability is the type I error rate. A
level-α test bounds it by α throughout the null. A p value evaluates the observed data under
the null; power evaluates a decision rule across repeated datasets at a specified parameter.

the null SD and specified-model SD, respectively; discrete tail probabilities need not equal the
target level.
Paired-data assumptions. Real ears from the same person can be dependent. The exercise’s
independent continuous measurements are sufficient for its derivation; independent people with
exchangeable left/right measurements and no ties are also sufficient. Do not infer exchangeability
from equal marginals alone. If ties occur, the simple no-ties derivation does not apply directly.
The usual sign test drops ties and uses the number of non-tied pairs; this requires fair signs
conditional on being non-tied. There are no ties in the supplied data.

Hypothesis conventions. The book computes the free-throw curves using the equality null.
This handout makes the directional formulation explicit: H0 : θH ≤ θA versus H1 : θH > θ A,
calibrated at the same equality boundary. Its rejection curve is unchanged; the away-better
region is now part of the directional null. For the sign test, independent continuous measurements
with the same distribution within each pair imply q = 1/2, the boundary of our directional null
q ≤ 1/2.
