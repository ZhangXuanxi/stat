expectations term by term. Emphasize that x    ei and me are not independent; independence is

calculation is the proof. Unbiasedness of ve does not imply unbiasedness of ve.

Teaching points for Exercise 9.3. Part (a) targets a joint intention/preference category
among all people, so use denominator 150. The true SE contains the unknown αR ; the numerical
SE uses the observed fraction. Part (b) changes the target to a conditional proportion among
voters, so normalize weighted counts by 45. The turnout assumption is conditional independence
of turnout and party preference given the intention category. The weighted counts are predictions,
not observed election totals.

Teaching points for Example 9.45. The error tolerance 0.01 is an absolute proportion
error, not a 1% relative error. Distinguish a variance upper bound from a planning guess, and
always round sample sizes upward. The exact binomial check is for θ = 0.05 only; evaluating the
sum can be supplied by the instructor. The preprint’s integer upper limit 110 should be 109 for
the stated tolerance, and its displayed “0.959%” has an incorrect percent sign. The corrected
event used here has probability 0.95319.

Closing checks. Why does the sample variance divide by n − 1? How does quadrupling n
change SE and CI width? What is random in a confidence interval statement?
