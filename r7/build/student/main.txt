Quick checks: A larger sample reduces SE but does not automatically remove sampling bias.
Higher confidence gives a wider interval; quadrupling the sample size approximately halves its
width.

Problem 2: Participation (Exercise 9.3)
Independent work: 10 minutes. A poll records party preference and stated intention to vote:

                          Intention            Democrat    Republican
                          Certain to vote             20            30
                          Likely to vote              40            10
                          Not likely to vote          30            20

A separate study gives turnout probabilities 0.8, 0.1, and 0 for these three intention categories,
respectively.

(a) Let αR be the population fraction in the joint category “certain to vote and Republican.”
    Assume independent, uniform sampling with replacement. Construct an unbiased estimator,
    derive its true standard error, and compute its observed value. Also give a numerical
    estimate of the SE.

(b) Estimate the Republican share among those who actually vote, using the turnout probabilities.
    State the assumptions that make this adjustment appropriate.

Problem 3: Planning the sample size (Example 9.45)
Independent work: 8 minutes. We estimate a population prevalence θ by testing n people selected
independently and uniformly with replacement. Treat test results as accurate i.i.d. Bernoulli(θ)
measurements. We want a 95% confidence interval with margin of error at most 0.01 (one
percentage point). Use the normal approximation with 1.96.

(a) Without knowing θ, use θ(1 − θ) ≤ 1/4 to determine a sufficient sample size for this planning
    rule.

(b) If we plan at θ = 0.05, how many tests does the rule require? Why is this smaller, and does
    it work for every possible prevalence?

(c) For the sample size in (b) and true θ = 0.05, let e   t count the positive tests. Identify its
    distribution and the integer values for which |t/n − 0.05| ≤ 0.01. Write the exact probability
                                                   e
    of meeting this error tolerance as a finite sum; no numerical evaluation by hand is required.

Problem 4 (backup): A polling margin
Independent work: 5 minutes. In a random sample of 1500 voters, 55% support a proposition and
45% oppose it. Model the responses as independent Bernoulli variables with support probability
θ. The population support-minus-opposition margin is d = θ − (1 − θ) = 2θ − 1.

(a) Express the margin estimator de in terms of the sample support proportion pe. Is it unbiased
    for d?

(b) Find its true standard error and estimate that SE from the data.

(c) Construct an approximate 95% confidence interval for d. Explain why treating the support
    and opposition proportions as independent would give the wrong standard error.
