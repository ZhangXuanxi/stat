                      Practice Problems for Midterm 2
1. (Hotel questionnaire) A hotel wants to determine average customer satisfaction using a
   questionnaire that asks customers for a discrete score between 1 (worst) and 5 (best).
   20% of the customers are retired. The average score given by the retired customers is 2,
   and they answer the questionnaire with probability 0.9. The average score given by the
   rest of the customers is 4, and they answer the questionnaire with probability 0.25. We
   define a Bernoulli random variable r̃ to represent whether a customer is retired (r̃ = 1)
   or not (r̃ = 0), another Bernoulli random variable q̃ to represent whether they answer the
   questionnaire (q̃ = 1) or not (q̃ = 0), and a discrete random variable s̃ to represent the
   score. We assume that q̃ and s̃ are conditionally independent given r̃.

    a) What is the mean of the score s̃?
    b) What is the mean of the observed score, i.e. the conditional mean of s̃ given q̃ = 1?

2. (Faulty database) An entry in a database can be modeled as a Gaussian random variable
   ã with zero mean and variance σ 2 . Due to a programming error, for a certain fixed
   constant x, the entry is replaced by −x with probability 0.1 and x with probability 0.1,
   independently of the content of the entry. Otherwise, the entry remains the same. We
   model the resulting entry as a random variable b̃.

    a) Compute the variance of b̃.
    b) Derive the minimum mean-squared error estimator of ã given b̃ = b, for all possible
       values of b.

3. (Weight-loss supplement) In order to evaluate the effectiveness of a weight-loss supple-
   ment, we record the weight gain/loss in kilograms after one month of a treatment group
   that takes the supplement, and a control group that doesn’t. In bold, we highlight the
   participants who report that they exercise daily.
   Supplement: 0.5, -4.6, -7.5, 1.1, -3.4, -1.4, 3.2, -1.9, -3.6, 2.4, 1.2, 4.2, -2.5, -3.5, 4.7
   No supplement: 1.6, -6.7, 2.3, -4.5, 3.4, -0.6, 2.3, 1.5, 1.7, 2.6, 0.5, 1.2, -3.6, 2.1, -1.2,
   0.4

    a) Compute the observed average treatment effect (ATE) given by the difference in
       average weight gain/loss between the treatment and control groups.
    b) Estimate the ATE making any adjustments that you think are reasonable. Explain
       under what assumptions your estimate approximates the true ATE.

   it is impossible.

6. (Noisy measurement) We are interested in measuring a certain quantity modeled by a
   random variable x̃ with zero mean and unit variance. Our available measurements ỹ :=
   x̃ + z̃ are corrupted by additive random noise, modeled as a random variable z̃ with zero
   mean and variance σ 2 , which is independent from x̃.
    a) What is the best linear estimator of x̃ given ỹ = y?
    b) What is the corresponding mean squared error?
    c) What happens to the estimator and the error when σ → 0? Explain why this makes
       sense.
    d) What happens to the estimator and the error when σ → ∞? Explain why this makes
       sense.

 9. (Online poll) In online polls, young people are often overrepresented. In this problem we
    show how to correct for this. Our goal is to analyze an online poll for a fictitious election
    where there are two candidates, a Democrat and a Republican. The poll consists of n1
    young people and n2 old people. We denote by y and o the number of young and old
    Democrat voters in the poll, respectively. We assume that the fraction of young voters in
    the population, denoted by α, is known.
      a) Derive an estimator of the proportion of Democrat voters in the election, as a function
         of y, o, n1 and n2 . As a hint, it may be useful to consider the population parameters
         θyoung and θold , representing the proportion of Democrats among young and old voters
         in the population, respectively.
      b) Apply your estimator to a poll where 60 out of 100 participants intend to vote for
         the Democratic candidate. 70 out of the 100 participants are young, and 50 of these
         70 are Democratic voters. The fraction of young voters in the overall population is
         25%.
      c) Under what assumptions is your estimator unbiased? Justify your answer mathe-
         matically.
      d) Show that your estimator is consistent as n1 → ∞ and n2 → ∞.
